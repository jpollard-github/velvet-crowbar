# Roadmap

The sequence protects the editorial boundary before adding reach or automation.
Phase 0 is a verified foundation, not a claim of production readiness.

## Phase 0 — foundation

Single-app publication, safe visibility model, one-editor studio, Markdown,
sample translations and essay, revisions, deliberate publication review,
environment separation, tests, documentation, and safe source archive.

Phase 0 may later receive a quiet technical production deployment with the four
safe seed entries solely to validate real infrastructure and the private
editorial workflow. It is not promoted as a mature publication, and attaching
the domain is not itself an editorial launch. No deployment is part of Phase
0.1.

## Phase 1 — capture and editorial ergonomics

Improve private archive browsing, private-only tags, fuller revision restore,
explicit purge, full-text private search, delayed-publication reminders, and the
workflow: capture private source → classify → draft → generalize → review →
preview → publish → preserve revision → revisit after time. Never automate
publication.

Publication forms may expand across translations, polished software/organization
essays, short observations, principles and glossary entries, manifestos, private
workplace autopsies, and sanitized public derivatives.

Candidate series labels—not required navigation—include Operational Weather, The
Calm Record, Minutes from the Hall of Mirrors, Corporate Translation Manual, The
Delivery Department, No Further Context Was Provided, Pending Required Review,
The Process Is Active, Bounded Observations, and Quiet Escalations.

## Phase 2 — public launch and content depth

Deliberately launch and promote the publication only after reviewing production
operations and preparing a deeper editorial set. Then consider:

- The Difference Between Contributing to Delivery and Enabling Delivery
- When Modularity Buries the Coupling
- A Green Pipeline Can Still Be a Dead Runtime
- Knowledge as Infrastructure, Not Status
- The Process Is Active
- Human in the Loop, Nobody at the Wheel
- Why Every Repository Boundary Is Also a Coordination Boundary
- The Delay Has a Name
- Readiness Is Not Correctness
- A Diagram Won
- Everyone Owns Delivery, Therefore Nobody Enables It
- The Artificial Pond Problem
- Process as a Prosthetic for Judgment
- The Treadmill Has Excellent Governance

## Phase 3 — source to sanitized derivative

Add explicit source-to-derivative lineage, a guided generalization stage, delayed
review, and stronger preservation. Explore app-layer field encryption,
export/import of private content, encrypted backups, and restore drills. “Stored
in a database” still does not mean end-to-end encrypted.

## Phase 4 — discovery, collections, and sharing

Consider public search, tags, series pages, richer Atom/RSS, share cards, random
entry, related pieces, glossary, curated collections, yearly/thematic
compilations, and printable/PDF/book export.

## Phase 5 — selected AI-assisted editorial tools

Opt-in possibilities: classification, redaction suggestions, fingerprint-risk
checklist assistance, alternative calm-language drafts, translation variants,
structural pattern extraction, duplicate detection, and editorial critique.

Permanent boundaries: Jason remains final authority; private content is not sent
to external AI by default; sanitization is never claimed automatic; publication
is never automatic; AI is never described as a witness; model-generated
accusations are prohibited.

## Phase 6 — cross-site integration and experiments

Consider selective links from `jasonpollard.com` and `arcadeghosts.org`,
portfolio-safe excerpts, and a “Notes on Software Delivery” bridge. Do not
cross-post automatically or require recruiters to encounter private or sharpest
material.

## Deliberately deferred product features

Newsletter, submissions, comments, multiple editors, roles, invitations, social
login, public API, analytics, monetization, paid content, moderation, image
uploads, object storage, custom domains beyond `velvetcrowbar.work`, mobile app,
current-workplace imports, browser extensions, rich text, microservices, and
external AI calls are outside Phase 0.
