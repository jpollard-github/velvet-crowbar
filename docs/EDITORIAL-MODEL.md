# Editorial model

## Three layers

1. **Portable public language**: a calm sentence that can travel without its
   originating incident.
2. **Concealed translation and structural analysis**: the underlying incentive,
   ambiguity, ownership transfer, or system pattern. This may be publishable
   after review.
3. **Identifiable source and workplace autopsy**: raw notes, distinctive
   incidents, organizations, people, proprietary names, and evidence. This is
   private by default.

The publication’s unit of value is the durable insight, not the incident that
provoked it.

## Kinds and visibility

Phase 0 kinds are `translation`, `essay`, `observation`, `autopsy`, `manifesto`,
and `fragment`. Kinds describe form; they do not grant visibility.

Visibility has exactly three states:

- `private`: raw source, autopsy, fragment, or anything not prepared.
- `draft`: editorial work in progress, still authenticated-only.
- `public`: explicitly reviewed and anonymously readable.

Every new entry is private. `unlisted` does not exist. Moving a public entry to
draft/private removes it from all anonymous surfaces.

In Phase 0.1, only `translation` and `essay` have anonymous routes and may be
public. Observations, autopsies, manifestos, and fragments may be private or
draft only. A historical unsupported public row is visible in the studio for
correction but is excluded from every anonymous query.

A private or draft translation may contain any subset of its four structured
fields. This supports capture without invented filler. A public translation
must contain a polite sentence, translation, system underneath, and useful
principle; Markdown body text does not substitute for a structured field.

## Sanitization is generalization

Changing “Morgan” to “a manager” may preserve an identifying role combination,
sequence, count, internal URL, or unusual failure. That is renaming, not
sanitization. Generalization removes the fingerprint and preserves the portable
mechanism.

Portable: “A dependency without an owner was transferred to the person waiting
on it.” Identifying: a named team, exact approval chain, unusual technical
failure, precise count, date, and internal system label.

## Publication review

Before public visibility, the editor explicitly reviews names, employer/client
identity, internal URLs, role combinations, unusual incidents, exact counts,
competence or mental-health judgments, motive claims, and proprietary details.
The system stores acknowledgment time and authenticated editor identity. The
checklist is not a guarantee of anonymity, legal safety, or correctness.

First publication records publication and review chronology. Every save that
remains public requires a fresh acknowledgment and updates review time/editor
without changing the original `publishedAt`. Withdrawal retains that historical
time while hiding the entry. Deliberate republication requires another
acknowledgment and assigns a new `publishedAt`; the append-only revision history
remains intact.

## Authorship and AI

Jason Pollard owns the observations and is the author, editor, publisher, and
final authority. Public language may say: “Written and edited by Jason Pollard,
with AI-assisted drafting and editorial pressure-testing.”

AI may later assist with opt-in classification, drafting, or critique. It did not
witness, verify, or report workplace events. No private content is sent to an
external AI by default, no model publishes, and no model-generated accusation is
permitted.
